# Strategy and positioning

Use this when the user is starting out, feels stuck, is changing direction, or asks for a content strategy. Strategy answers five questions in order: who is it for, why would they follow, what will be made, where, and how success is judged.

## 1. Niche and audience

A workable niche sits where three things overlap:

- **Credibility**: what the creator has done, knows, or is visibly learning in public.
- **Energy**: what they can talk about for years without forcing it.
- **Demand**: what a real group of people already searches for, watches or pays for.

To narrow a broad niche, add one or more qualifiers: a specific person (new managers, first-time mothers), a constraint (on a small budget, in 20 minutes), a context (in Egypt, for remote teams), or a point of view (no-equipment, anti-hustle).

Describe the audience as one person, not a demographic band:

- Who they are and what stage they are at.
- The problem or desire that brings them to this content.
- What they have already tried and why it did not work.
- The words they use for the problem (collect these from comments, forums, reviews and messages; they become hooks).
- Where they spend time and what they already follow.

If the creator serves a business goal, the audience is the buyer, not the largest possible crowd. A small, right audience beats a large, wrong one.

## 2. Positioning statement

Write one sentence the user can put in a bio and test every idea against:

> I help **[specific person]** **[get result / avoid problem]** through **[format or approach]**, without **[the thing they dislike]**.

Then answer "why this creator?" in one line: experience, results, access, personality or an unusual angle. If there is no answer yet, "documenting the journey" is a legitimate position: they share what they are learning as they do it, and claim no expertise they do not have.

## 3. Content pillars

Pillars are the three to five recurring themes that make an account predictable in a good way. Each pillar should link to the positioning and to a goal.

A reliable mix:

| Pillar type | Purpose | Example for a home baker selling courses |
|---|---|---|
| Teach | Reach and trust: solve a specific problem | "Why your sourdough is flat" |
| Show | Proof: results, process, behind the scenes | A day of orders from start to finish |
| Believe | Differentiation: opinions and principles | "Stop buying expensive flour first" |
| Connect | Relationship: personal stories, community | The bake that failed before a big order |
| Offer | Conversion: what they sell and who it is for | Student results, course walkthrough |

Rough weighting for an account that also sells: most content teaching and showing, a steady minority on belief and connection, and a small but regular share of direct offers. Adjust to the goal: more offers during a launch, more teaching while building reach.

For each pillar, define two or three repeatable **series** (a named, recognisable format such as "60-second fix" or "client teardown"). Series cut idea time, train the audience to return and make batching easy.

## 4. Voice

Capture voice so that anything written sounds like the creator. Gather two or three pieces they made that they like, then note:

- **Three adjectives** (for example: direct, warm, dry-humoured).
- **We say / we never say**: phrases, greetings, sign-offs, banned words and cliches.
- **Sentence habits**: short or long, questions, slang, dialect, emoji use.
- **Stance**: teacher, peer, coach, challenger, entertainer.
- **Stories and references they return to.**

A fill-in voice guide is in `templates.md`. When no samples exist, write a short piece in two contrasting voices and let the user pick.

## 5. Goals and the content funnel

Different content does different jobs. Label each piece with one.

| Stage | Job | Content that does it | Signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Discover | Reach new people | Broad-appeal hooks, trends adapted to the niche, searchable how-tos, collaborations | Views from non-followers, shares |
| Trust | Turn viewers into followers | Deeper teaching, series, opinions, stories, proof | Follows per view, saves, watch time, returning viewers |
| Act | Turn followers into leads or buyers | Case studies, demos, offers, FAQs, objections answered | Link clicks, replies, messages, sign-ups, sales |
| Stay | Keep customers and fans | Community posts, behind the scenes, live sessions, newsletter | Replies, repeat purchase, referrals |

Pick one primary goal for the next 90 days and one metric for it. Give every piece one call to action that matches its stage: follow, save or share, comment, reply, sign up, buy. An account that only does discovery gets views without income; one that only sells stops reaching anyone.

Send people somewhere the creator owns (an email list, a community) as early as practical. Reach on a platform can change overnight; a list cannot be taken away.

## 6. Choosing platforms and formats

Choose by where the audience already is, what the creator is good at, and what the goal needs.

- **One primary platform** where most effort goes, **one secondary** fed mainly by repurposing. Add more only when the first two run smoothly.
- **Short video** is strongest for discovery. **Long video, podcasts and newsletters** build depth and trust. **Text and carousels** suit teaching and professional audiences. **Search-led formats** (YouTube, blogs, Pinterest) keep working long after posting.
- Match format to the creator: someone who dislikes being on camera can run voiceover, screen recordings, text and carousels well.

Platform-by-platform notes are in `platform-playbooks.md`.

## 7. Sizing cadence to capacity

Work backwards from the hours available. Typical time per finished piece for a solo creator (adjust to their real pace):

| Piece | Rough time |
|---|---|
| Simple talking-head short video | 30 to 60 minutes including edit |
| Edited short with b-roll and captions | 1.5 to 3 hours |
| Carousel | 1 to 2 hours |
| Text post or thread | 20 to 45 minutes |
| Long video (8 to 15 minutes) | 6 to 15 hours |
| Newsletter issue | 2 to 4 hours |

Add time for ideas, replying to comments and reviewing analytics (plan for a fifth of the total). Then set the cadence at roughly 80 percent of what fits, so a bad week does not break the streak. State the arithmetic so the user can adjust it.

A sustainable minimum on most short-form platforms is three to five posts a week. More helps only if quality holds.

## 8. The 90-day plan

Structure a strategy as three phases with a review at the end of each.

- **Days 1 to 30, foundation and testing**: fix profile and bio, set up tracking, publish across all pillars and several formats on purpose to see what the audience responds to. Aim for volume of experiments.
- **Days 31 to 60, focus**: keep the two or three pillars and formats that performed, turn the best into series, start the secondary platform by repurposing, begin collecting emails.
- **Days 61 to 90, convert and systemise**: add regular offer content, collaborations, batch production, templates and a monthly review habit.

For each phase state: what gets published, the one metric being watched, and what decision the review will make. Give expectations as ranges with assumptions, never as promises.

## 9. Common strategy mistakes

- **Too broad**: content for "everyone" gives no one a reason to follow.
- **Copying a bigger creator's format without their context**: they have an audience that forgives a slow start.
- **Changing niche every two weeks**: a direction needs 20 to 30 posts before it can be judged.
- **All teaching, no personality or proof**: easy to consume, easy to forget.
- **No path to the goal**: no link, no offer, no reason to act.
- **A plan bigger than the creator's week**: leads to burnout and silence.
- **Judging by followers alone**: a small account with buyers is a business; a large one without them is a hobby.
