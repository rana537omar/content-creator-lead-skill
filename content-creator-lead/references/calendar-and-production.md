## 1. Building a calendar

A calendar is a set of decisions made in advance so that the creator never faces a blank page on posting day.

Steps:

1. **Fix the inputs**: goal for the period, pillars, platforms, cadence sized to capacity (see `strategy-and-positioning.md`).
2. **Place the anchors first**: launches, events, seasonal dates, collaborations, the weekly long-form or newsletter slot.
3. **Assign recurring slots**: give each weekday a pillar or series (for example Monday teach, Wednesday story, Friday proof). This removes daily decisions.
4. **Fill with specific pieces**: each row gets a working hook, not a vague topic.
5. **Balance**: check the mix across pillars, funnel stages and formats. Include regular offer content.
6. **Leave room**: keep one or two flexible slots a week for trends, reactions and timely ideas.
7. **Plan repurposing in**: mark which pieces derive from which source.

Deliver the calendar as a table with these columns:

| Date | Platform | Format | Pillar | Hook or title | What happens | Goal | Call to action | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Drop columns that carry no information for this user (Platform when there is only one, Status in a plan nothing has been made for yet).

Plan in detail two to four weeks ahead; beyond that, plan themes only, since results should change what gets made. When asked for a month, give all rows with real hooks, then offer to write out the scripts and captions.

Use real dates when the user gives a start date. Account for their local weekend, holidays and cultural or religious seasons relevant to the audience.

## 2. Weekly rhythm examples

**Solo creator, about 5 hours a week, short video first**

- 3 short videos (2 teach, 1 story or proof)
- 1 carousel or text post made from the week's best idea
- One 1.5-hour session to script and film, one 1.5-hour session to edit, design and schedule (3 hours)
- 10 minutes of replies and engaging in the niche on six days (1 hour)
- 20 minutes for ideas and the weekly numbers
- Total about 4 hours 20 minutes, leaving slack for a bad week
- From the second month, upload the same videos clean to a second platform (about 15 minutes a week from the slack)

**Business owner, about 8 hours a week, leads as the goal**

- 1 long source piece a week (video, podcast or article)
- 3 to 4 short clips and 1 carousel derived from it
- 2 LinkedIn or text posts
- 1 newsletter
- 1 offer or case study post

**Small team (creator plus editor and designer)**

- Creator: ideas, filming, voice, final approval (4 to 6 hours)
- Editor: all video editing and clipping
- Designer: thumbnails, carousels, templates
- Lead or manager: calendar, briefs, scheduling, community, reporting

State the time arithmetic for whichever rhythm you propose.

## 3. Batching

Grouping the same kind of work saves the cost of switching:

- **Idea session**: fill the idea bank and pick the next two weeks.
- **Scripting session**: write hooks and scripts for all picked pieces.
- **Filming session**: record everything in one sitting. Change top or location between videos if variety matters. Film extra hooks for the same video to test.
- **Editing session**: edit, caption, export.
- **Scheduling session**: write captions, upload, schedule, prepare covers.

A monthly cycle that works for many: week 1 plan and script, week 2 film, week 3 edit, week 4 schedule and review. Smaller creators can run the same cycle weekly.

Keep a small buffer of finished evergreen pieces for sick days and busy weeks.

## 4. The production pipeline

Every piece moves through fixed stages; track the stage in the calendar's status column.

1. **Idea** – in the bank, with hook and pillar
2. **Brief or script** – written and approved
3. **Produce** – filmed, recorded, designed or drafted
4. **Edit** – cut, captioned, proofread
5. **Review** – checked against the quality bar and brand rules
6. **Schedule** – caption, cover, tags, link, time
7. **Publish and engage** – reply during the first hours
8. **Measure** – numbers logged after a set period (for example 7 days)
9. **Repurpose or retire** – winners get follow-ups and new formats

Pre-publish checklist: hook works in the first line or seconds; captions and spelling checked; correct ratio and length; cover or thumbnail set; link works; call to action present; sponsorship disclosed if applicable; rights cleared for music and visuals; scheduled for the intended account and time.

## 5. Roles and delegation

What only the creator can do: choose ideas, be the face and voice, share real stories and opinions, give final approval. Almost everything else can be delegated or templated.

Order in which most creators delegate:

1. Video editing and clipping
2. Design (thumbnails, carousels)
3. Scheduling, uploading, captions
4. Community management with saved replies and escalation rules
5. Research and first-draft writing
6. Strategy and channel management

For each delegated task, write a short standard procedure: purpose, steps, examples of good output, tools and file locations, what done means, turnaround time, and who approves. A file naming rule and a shared folder structure (raw, working, final, published) prevent most confusion:

`YYYY-MM-DD_platform_pillar_short-title_v1`

## 6. Briefing

A good brief lets someone produce the piece without a call. Include:

- **Objective**: what the piece is for and how success is judged
- **Audience**: who it is for
- **Key message**: the one point
- **Format and specs**: platform, ratio, length, deliverables
- **Hook and structure**: script or outline
- **Style references**: links to examples and what to take from each
- **Assets**: where the footage, logos, fonts and brand rules live
- **Must include / must avoid**
- **Deadline and review rounds**

Brief formats for editors, designers and writers are in `templates.md`.

## 7. Review and feedback

Give feedback the way a good lead does:

- Start from the goal: "this is meant to earn saves; the takeaway slide needs to stand alone".
- Be specific about location and change: timestamp, slide number, line.
- Separate **must fix** from **suggestion**.
- Explain why, so the next piece arrives better.
- Limit rounds (usually two) and consolidate comments into one message.
- Note what worked, so it is repeated.

When the user asks for a review of their own draft, give the verdict first, then the top three fixes in priority order, then a revised version.

## 8. Launch and campaign calendars

For a product launch, event or campaign, plan in phases:

| Phase | Typical length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Seed | 2 to 4 weeks before | Teach around the problem, share the journey, ask questions, build a waitlist |
| Warm-up | 1 to 2 weeks before | Announce, behind the scenes, proof, objections, early access |
| Open | Launch days | Clear offer, demos, testimonials, FAQs, live session, daily reminders |
| Close | Last 24 to 72 hours | Real deadline, last proof, final call |
| After | 1 to 2 weeks | Thank you, results, customer stories, lessons, back to regular content |

Increase frequency during the open and close phases and tell the audience plainly what is happening. Keep some regular value content running throughout.

## 9. Tools

Recommend categories, and name tools only as examples the user can swap:

- **Planning**: a spreadsheet or a database-style workspace for the calendar and idea bank
- **Scheduling**: the platforms' own schedulers or a third-party scheduler
- **Video**: a mobile or desktop editor with auto-captions
- **Design**: a template-based design tool
- **Storage**: a shared drive with the folder structure above
- **Analytics**: native platform insights plus one tracking sheet

Start with the simplest set that works. A spreadsheet and the native apps are enough for most solo creators.

## 10. Avoiding burnout

- Set the cadence below maximum capacity.
- Keep a buffer of finished pieces.
- Use series and templates so that not every piece starts from zero.
- Schedule weeks off and tell the audience.
- Separate creating from checking numbers; review analytics on a set day, not after every post.
- When energy drops, reduce frequency before reducing quality, and lean on repurposing.
