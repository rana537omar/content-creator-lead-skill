## 1. Where good ideas come from

Ideas pulled from the audience beat ideas invented at a desk. Sources, in rough order of reliability:

- **Questions people actually ask**: comments, direct messages, sales calls, support tickets, community posts, the search bar's autocomplete, "people also ask" boxes, forums.
- **The creator's own past hits**: any post well above their median deserves a sequel, a deeper version, an opposite take and a format change.
- **Outliers in the niche**: posts from similar-sized accounts that did several times their usual numbers. The topic or packaging struck a nerve; study why, then make an original piece on the same nerve.
- **The creator's real work and life**: a client problem solved this week, a mistake, a result, a decision, a process.
- **Objections and myths**: what the audience believes that holds them back; what stops them buying.
- **Timely hooks**: news, seasons, launches, events, trends that genuinely connect to the niche.

When you have web access, look at current search suggestions and recent top posts in the niche. When you do not, say so and work from the durable sources above.

## 2. Turning a topic into angles

One topic produces many pieces. Run it through these lenses:

| Lens | Example for the topic "meal prep" |
|---|---|
| How-to | "Prep five lunches in 40 minutes" |
| Mistake | "The reason your meal prep tastes bad by Wednesday" |
| Myth or contrarian | "You don't need to prep on Sunday" |
| List | "4 sauces that make any prep better" |
| Story | "I prepped every meal for 30 days. Here's what changed" |
| Comparison | "Glass or plastic containers: tested for a month" |
| Beginner path | "If I started meal prep today, I'd do only this" |
| Behind the scenes | "What my fridge looks like after a prep day" |
| Teardown or reaction | "Fixing a follower's meal prep" |
| Question to the audience | "What's the one meal you never get tired of?" |
| Proof | "What a client saved in a month by prepping" |
| Tool or resource | "The free template I plan my prep with" |

Also vary the **size**: the 20-second tip, the 60-second walkthrough, the carousel, the long video, the newsletter deep dive. That is the basis of repurposing.

## 3. Judging an idea before making it

Score each idea quickly; make the ones that pass at least three:

- **Relevance**: does the target person care about this today?
- **Tension**: is there curiosity, a stake, a surprise or a strong opinion?
- **Specificity**: is it about one precise thing?
- **Shareability**: would someone send it to a friend or save it? Why?
- **Credibility**: can this creator say it with proof or experience?
- **Goal fit**: does it serve the current goal and a pillar?

Then write the title or hook first. If no strong hook comes, the idea is not ready: sharpen the angle or drop it.

## 4. Hooks: what they must do

The hook is the first line of text or the first one to three seconds of video. It has to do three things at once:

1. **Call the right person** (they recognise themselves or their problem).
2. **Open a gap** (a question, a promise, a surprise they want resolved).
3. **Be true** (the piece must pay it off; bait that does not deliver costs retention and trust).

Rules that hold across platforms:

- Specific beats vague: "I lost 4 clients in one week" beats "business is hard".
- Start in the middle: skip greetings, names, and "in this video".
- Use the audience's words, not industry terms.
- Keep it short enough to read or hear in about two seconds.
- Write five to ten options for any piece that matters, then pick.

## 5. Hook patterns with examples

Adapt the pattern to the creator's voice; do not paste them unchanged. Replace every bracket with something specific.

| Pattern | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Direct result | How to [result] in [time/constraint] | "How to film a week of content in one hour" |
| Mistake | You're [doing X] wrong / the mistake that [cost] | "This one setting is making your videos look cheap" |
| Contrarian | Stop [common advice]. Do [alternative] | "Stop posting every day. Do this instead" |
| Curiosity gap | The [thing] nobody tells you about [topic] | "The part of freelancing nobody shows you" |
| Number list | [N] [things] that [benefit] | "3 questions I ask before every client call" |
| Story open | [Time marker], [unexpected event] | "Last month a client fired me. Best thing that happened this year" |
| Call-out | If you're a [person] who [situation], [watch/read] this | "If you're a new manager and your team has gone quiet, read this" |
| Before and after | From [bad state] to [good state] | "This page went from 2 enquiries a month to 30" |
| Question | [Question the audience is already asking]? | "Why does your bread never rise?" |
| Proof first | [Result]. Here's how | "This post brought 40 leads. Here's the exact structure" |
| Warning | Don't [action] until you [check] | "Don't sign a brand deal until you check this clause" |
| Comparison | [A] vs [B]: [what was tested] | "Phone vs camera: can you tell?" |
| Challenge | I tried [thing] for [time] | "I wrote one post a day for 30 days" |
| Shortcut | The fastest way to [result] | "The fastest way to find 20 video ideas" |

Check each against the creator's niche for honesty. Do not write "nobody tells you" for something everyone tells you, and never invent the number or the result.

## 6. Visual and on-screen hooks

On video, what is seen in the first second matters as much as what is said.

- **Movement or change** in the first frame: walking in, placing an object, a cut, a reveal.
- **Show the result first**, then explain how.
- **On-screen text** stating the promise in six words or fewer, readable without sound.
- **An unusual setting, prop or framing** that makes someone pause.
- **A pattern break** within the first few seconds: a zoom, a change of angle, a prop, a sound.

On carousels and images, the first slide is the hook: a short, bold headline with one idea and plenty of space. On text posts, the first line must stand alone before the "see more" cut.

## 7. Trends and competitor research

**Trends**: use a trend only if it can carry a point from the niche. Ask what the format is (a sound, a structure, a joke shape), then put niche content inside it. Move quickly; a trend's useful life is days. Skip trends that clash with the brand or touch sensitive events.

**Competitor and peer research**: build a list of ten to twenty accounts (a mix of similar-sized and larger). For each, look for:

- Their outliers: posts far above their usual numbers, and what those share (topic, hook, format, length).
- Recurring series and how they are structured.
- Questions in their comments that were never answered: those are ideas.
- Gaps: audiences, formats or viewpoints nobody covers well.

The output of research is a list of topics and structures worth trying in the creator's own voice. Do not copy scripts, visuals or wording; take the lesson and make something original.

## 8. Building an idea bank

Keep one running list so that no session starts from a blank page. Per idea, record: working title or hook, pillar, format, source (where the idea came from), goal stage, and status. A table for this is in `templates.md`.

When the user asks for ideas, deliver them in that shape: grouped by pillar, each with a hook, a format, and a one-line note on what happens in the piece. Mark the three you would make first and say why. Ten strong, specific ideas are more useful than fifty generic ones.
